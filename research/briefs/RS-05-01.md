## Answer in 5 lines

1. RecipeDB's own process vocabulary is public: Bagler 2022, Table S2, lists **270 "processing action" cubits that appear in RecipeDB** [V]. The main text of the same paper says **268 processes** [V]. That 2-term discrepancy is recorded, not resolved.
2. The list mixes true processes with inflections, endpoints, tools, ingredients and non-process words. A v0 ontology must classify each term before giving it an ID.
3. FoodOn models processing on **two axes, mechanism vs objective** (Dooley & Naravane 2023) [V]. That answers v4.0 Part Z Q22–23 (process names that encode tools or endpoints) directly, so it is adopted as a column.
4. External crosswalks exist but are not needed for v0: FoodOn `FOODON_03450002` "food cooking" [V]; FoodEx2 facet **F28 Process** with branch `A0BA1` [V]; LanguaL facet G cooking method [C]. Per AH2 they are optional columns (`UNRESOLVED_EXTERNAL` until checked).
5. **Decision enabled:** build `PROC-` v0 now from the RecipeDB list + the master prompt's §B05 "escape" processes, with every classification **PROPOSED** for human audit (SX-05-02).

## Sub-questions and findings

**SQ1 — What is RecipeDB's process inventory?**
- [V] RecipeDB3: "over 118,000 recipes … 6 continents, 26 geo-cultural regions, and 74 countries … 268 processes … over 23,500 ingredients" (Bagler, *A generative grammar of cooking*, arXiv:2211.09059v1, 12 Oct 2022, p. 2).
- [V] The same paper lists cubit inventories: "18,599 ingredient names, 71 quantity terms, 1299 forms, 270 processing actions, over 100 descriptors of processing actions, and 69 utensils". Table S2 lists all 270 processing actions; Table S3 lists 69 utensils; Table S4 lists 71 units.
- Note: 23,500 ingredients (main text) vs 18,599 ingredient-name cubits (SI), and 268 vs 270 processes. These are probably different counting bases; unverified [U].

**SQ2 — How do existing ontologies structure processes?**
- [V] FoodOn proposes two parallel hierarchies under material processing. "Material processing by mechanism" has no completion endpoint (e.g. "heating of material", "heating by microwave", "direct heating of container"). "Material processing by objective" carries objective specifications that define completion (e.g. boiling as reaching a heating threshold; steaming rice → gelatinization). Source: Dooley D., Naravane T. (2023) *Ontological how and why: action and objective of planned processes in the food domain*, Front. Artif. Intell. 6:1137961, doi:10.3389/frai.2023.1137961.
- [V] FoodOn `FOODON_03450002` "food cooking" = "the transformation of raw food by the application of heat"; example `FOODON:03470175` "food blanching". Source: MIxS term `food_cooking_proc` (genomicsstandardsconsortium.github.io/mixs/0001202).
- [V] FoodEx2 facet **F28 "Process"**; `A0BA1` "Cooking and similar thermal preparation processes" = "thermal preparation steps not strictly related to preservation" (matvaretabellen.no FoodEx2 browser).
- [C] FoodEx2 descriptor `A07GP` "Steaming without pressure" (search snippet, page not opened).
- [C] LanguaL: facetted thesaurus; facet G = cooking method (langual.org; the full list was not fetched).

**SQ3 — What family hierarchy should v0 use?** Consensus across the above: separate
thermal by **medium** (water/steam, dry air/radiant, fat) from mechanical, chemical,
biological, cold, rest/hold, and assembly/transfer actions. This is a working
hierarchy, not a claim of physical completeness. It also matches the master
prompt's RS-05-02 list ("thermal wet/dry/fat, mechanical, chemical/acid,
biological, cold, rest").

## Consensus | Contested | Emerging | Unknown

- **Consensus:** process identity ≠ tool ≠ endpoint ≠ ingredient. Composite verbs (stir-fry, pressure-cook) should be factorised.
- **Contested:** whether endpoint-defined verbs (reduce, caramelize, thicken) are processes or states. v0 keeps them as processes with `axis=OBJECTIVE`, following FoodOn.
- **Emerging:** mechanism/objective dual classification (FoodOn 2023).
- **Unknown:** licence terms of FoodOn, FoodEx2 and LanguaL for redistribution [U]. The exact counting basis behind 268 vs 270 [U].
